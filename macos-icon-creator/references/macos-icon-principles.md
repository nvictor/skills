# Design Principles

## Metaphor and hierarchy

- Choose a primary object or scene that expresses the app's purpose through a bold, recognizable silhouette. Abstract ideas can become tools, instruments, surfaces, or layered symbols.
- Give supporting forms a job: clarify the metaphor, establish depth, or balance the composition. Remove a base, rim, halo, or accent if the icon becomes clearer without it.
- Establish hierarchy through size, shape, value, and color before detail. Multiple components can form one coherent object; do not give every component equal emphasis.

## Composition and joins

- Design the object and squircle together. Containment, inset, overlap, fusion, and controlled breakout are all valid when they serve the metaphor. A centered object can work; a generic badge behind an unrelated mark rarely adds meaning.
- Preserve recognizable contours and sufficient canvas clearance. Edge pressure should feel intentional, not accidentally cropped.
- Related forms should share deliberate axes, curves, or edge relationships. Avoid near-tangencies, slivers, and ambiguous overlaps that become bumps or seams when rasterized.
- For a continuous rounded stroke, prefer a single path with round line caps and appropriate joins over overlapping shapes that imitate end caps. Use separate shapes when the intended construction or silhouette requires them.
- When a feature should feel fused, consider sharing the base's geometry. An applied symbol can still be appropriate when it belongs to the object or is requested.
- Resolve a bad junction by adjusting the secondary form first. Reshape the dominant object only when its overall silhouette improves.

## Material, light, and color

- Choose a coherent lighting direction and material treatment. Highlights should describe curvature, a turned plane, or a material response; shadows should explain contact, overlap, or elevation.
- Different materials may reflect light differently while sharing the same scene. Consistency does not require identical gradients, corner radii, or shadow softness on every part.
- Use tonal separation and geometry to carry essential depth. Gradients, blur, and glow should support that structure rather than supply all of it.
- Keep a clear palette hierarchy. Integrate brand colors into the object when relevant, rather than relying on color alone for identity.

## Detail budget

- Judge identity at small sizes. Fine details may enrich larger renders, but recognition must survive their disappearance.
- Abstract interface elements into bold forms instead of reproducing screenshots or tiny chrome. Avoid text labels unless explicitly required; if required, ensure the icon works when they are unreadable.
- Simplify according to the concept. Neither ornament nor minimalism is an end in itself.
