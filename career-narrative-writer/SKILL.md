# Career Narrative Writer

## Purpose

Turn source-backed experience into concise narratives that demonstrate judgment, impact, and readiness. Produce career material rather than generic summaries: show what was difficult, what the person contributed, what changed, and why the work matters.

Do not invent metrics, scope, authority, organizational impact, or outcomes. Ask for missing context only when a material claim cannot be written safely without it.

## Choose the narrative mode

Select the mode from the user's goal and source material. Combine modes when a technical story also needs to demonstrate leadership behavior.

### Behavioral mode

Use for behavioral interviews, leadership stories, manager readiness, performance conversations, and competency evidence.

Build the narrative from:

- situation and stakes
- behavioral tension, ambiguity, conflict, or risk
- the person's role and judgment
- actions, influence, and tradeoffs
- result and learning
- the leadership or readiness signal proved by the evidence

Make the person sound accountable without making them sound self-congratulatory. Keep the judgment more memorable than the conflict.

### Technical-system mode

Use for technical interviews, resumes, promotion material, project summaries, performance reviews, and positioning statements about systems work.

Build the narrative from:

- the operational problem
- the system boundary: teams, repositories, platforms, environments, services, or workflows
- the control model: standards, interfaces, ownership, or operating expectations
- the automation or platform mechanism
- governance and safety controls
- the operational outcome
- engineering leverage beyond the immediate implementation

Show systems thinking rather than listing tools. Explain how the work made a risky, manual, unclear, or expensive operating space more repeatable and governable.

## Choose the career lens

Use the target role or request to select a lens:

- **Manager:** emphasize priorities, delegation, coaching, accountability, team health, stakeholder alignment, decision quality, and conditions created for others to succeed.
- **Staff engineer:** emphasize technical direction, architecture judgment, influence without authority, cross-team leverage, standards, operational maturity, and better decisions beyond the person's own implementation.
- **Neutral leadership:** use when the target is unclear; note a lens shift only when it would help the user.

## Write the answer

Write in plain prose without visible framework headings unless the user asks for an outline.

- Default to one polished answer of 2–5 sentences.
- For spoken interview material, target 60–90 seconds.
- Use two short paragraphs only when one would be dense.
- Start with enough context to establish the problem, boundary, and stakes.
- Center the person's decisions, actions, influence, and tradeoffs.
- Close with the observable result, learning, leverage, or readiness signal.
- Use exact system, repository, tool, and platform names when available.
- Separate completed work from active or proposed work.
- Separate observed facts from inferred career signals.
- Prefer specific behavior and outcomes over abstract virtues or implementation trivia.
- Avoid blame, vague heroics, unsupported metrics, and adjectives such as `scalable`, `seamless`, `robust`, or `transformative` unless the specific behavior supports them.

## Optional formats

Use these only when requested or when structure materially helps.

### STAR, CAR, or SOAR

Keep each field compact. Put context and stakes in the opening field, decisions and influence in the action field, and observable change plus the career signal in the result field.

### Resume bullet

Use this internal shape:

```text
<Action> <problem or system> across <boundary> by <control model and mechanism>, reducing <risk or friction> and improving <source-backed outcome>.
```

### Competency mapping

Return:

```text
Competencies: <1–4 credible competencies>
What this answer must prove: <the central career signal>
Risk to avoid: <the likeliest credibility problem>
Follow-up probes: <3–5 likely questions>
```

### Story-bank extraction

Return:

```text
Story: <short label>
Best for: <themes, competencies, or formats>
Raw material: <source-backed facts>
Missing context: <material facts still needed>
Best mode and lens: <behavioral, technical-system, or combined; manager, staff engineer, or neutral>
```

## Final check

Before returning the answer, verify that:

- every material claim is supported by the source
- the person's role is distinguishable from the team's work
- the narrative shows judgment or systems thinking rather than chronology
- the result explains what changed without overclaiming
- the selected mode, lens, length, and format fit the user's purpose
- the prose is concise enough to use directly
