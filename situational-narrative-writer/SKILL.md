# Situational Narrative Writer

## Purpose

Use this skill to turn raw experience notes into career-ready situational narratives for behavioral interviews, SIG discussions, manager-readiness packets, leadership stories, performance conversations, and promotion preparation. The default output is one polished answer: either 2-5 sentences for written use or a 60-90 second spoken answer when the user asks for interview material.

The goal is to show judgment under real constraints: how the person understood the situation, chose a role, handled ambiguity or tension, influenced others, made tradeoffs, and created an outcome that proves readiness for the target competency.

## Writing Pattern

Write in plain prose. Move through these ideas without turning them into headings unless the user asks for a structured outline:

- Situation and stakes: what was happening, why it mattered, and who was affected.
- Behavioral tension: what was ambiguous, difficult, risky, misaligned, urgent, or politically sensitive.
- Role and judgment: what the person owned, decided, reframed, or chose not to do.
- Actions and influence: how they worked with partners, clarified expectations, coached, aligned, escalated, delegated, or led through constraints.
- Result and learning: what changed, what became easier or safer, what the person learned, or what operating habit remained afterward.
- Leadership signal: what the story proves about readiness, judgment, scope, influence, or follow-through.

Start with enough context to make the stakes clear. Then show the judgment and actions that distinguish the person from a passive participant. Close with the result, learning, or leadership signal.

## Output Rules

- Prefer one polished answer of 2-5 sentences, or a 60-90 second answer when the user wants spoken interview material.
- Use STAR, CAR, or SOAR only when requested, or when structure would clearly help the user compare stories.
- Keep claims source-backed. Do not invent metrics, promotion signals, organizational scope, authority, or business impact.
- Separate observed facts from inferred leadership signals.
- Ask for missing context only when needed to avoid inventing a material fact.
- Make the person sound accountable without making them sound self-congratulatory.
- Prefer specific behavior over abstract virtues such as ownership, collaboration, empathy, or strategic thinking.
- Avoid overclaiming, blaming others, vague heroics, unsupported metrics, and answers that make the conflict more memorable than the judgment.

## Lens Selection

Use the target role or user request to choose the lens.

Manager lens emphasizes team health, priorities, delegation, coaching, accountability, stakeholder alignment, decision quality, hiring or performance readiness, and how the person created conditions for others to succeed.

Staff engineer lens emphasizes technical direction, influence without authority, architecture judgment, cross-team leverage, standards, operational maturity, mentorship through systems, and how the person raised the quality of decisions beyond their own implementation.

When the lens is unclear, write the answer in a neutral leadership voice and briefly note how it could shift toward manager or staff engineer framing.

## Optional Formats

Only use these compact variants when the user asks for them.

STAR outline:

```text
Situation: <context, stakes, and people affected>
Task: <the responsibility, decision, or leadership challenge>
Action: <specific behaviors, influence, tradeoffs, and follow-through>
Result: <observable outcome, learning, or leadership signal>
```

CAR outline:

```text
Challenge: <the tension, ambiguity, risk, or conflict>
Action: <what the person did and why it was the right judgment>
Result: <what changed and what the story proves>
```

SOAR outline:

```text
Situation: <context and stakes>
Obstacle: <what made the situation difficult>
Action: <choices, influence, and execution>
Result: <outcome, learning, and readiness signal>
```

Competency mapping:

```text
Competencies: <1-4 competencies this story can credibly support>
What this answer must prove: <the core readiness signal>
Risk to avoid: <the most likely way the answer could weaken trust>
Follow-up probes: <3-5 likely interviewer questions>
```

Story-bank extraction:

```text
Story: <short descriptive label>
Best for: <competencies, interview themes, or readiness signals>
Raw material: <source-backed facts from the notes>
Missing context: <facts needed before writing a polished answer>
Best lens: <manager, staff engineer, or neutral leadership>
```

## Template

Use this as an internal drafting shape, not as a visible worksheet:

```text
In <situation>, <stakes or tension> affected <people, team, system, or decision>. My role was to <responsibility or leadership challenge>, so I <judgment, action, and influence through constraints>. That changed <observable result, operating habit, decision quality, team behavior, or readiness signal>, and it showed <competency or leadership trait> because <specific evidence rather than abstract praise>.
```
